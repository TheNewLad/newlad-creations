# Personal brand website — shaped brief

Status: Karim selected Open Studio with his original studio portrait in the
circle crop shown in `open-studio-original-circle-v1.png`.
Karim approved the preview and explicitly requested implementation. Publication
and deployment have not been requested.

## Job and audience

Let people get to know Karim by exploring what he makes and what interests him.
Invite people who relate to a project or thought to start a conversation. The
homepage's primary mode is Experience; project stories and notes use Read.

The audience can discover a shared interest without fitting a professional niche.
The site should accommodate someone arriving from a short video as well as a
person following a project link. These are proposed entry scenarios, not measured
traffic patterns.

## Outcome and proof

Within the first screen, visitors should meet Karim and see tangible examples of
his range. Exploration should lead naturally to a project story or short thought,
then a real follow/contact destination. Community is a future outcome of those
conversations; a community platform is not part of the proposed first version.

Initial content candidates:

- Home automation: a concrete problem, what he built, and a real demonstration.
- Project Drift: an experiment currently being tested, with a clear description
  and a play link only if Karim confirms public availability.
- Fell for Love: the wedding tool, what he tried, and what he is learning. Its
  future stays open; do not frame it as a success or as definitively over.
- Short notes or videos: one useful thought can stand alone.
- A concise personal introduction, with a longer story available by choice.

The existing packing-template story can remain accessible. It need not determine
the homepage's identity or its main action.

## Content structure

Navigation: **Creations · Notes · About · Say hello**. Karim confirmed
`karim@newladcreations.com` as the public contact address; Say hello uses mailto.

Homepage sequence:

1. Introduce Karim with a personal portrait, followed closely by real examples of
   what he is making.
2. Give two or three current/recent creations room to show their substance.
3. Offer a few short thoughts, with no requirement to turn them into essays.
4. Share enough personal context to make the work feel connected to a person.
5. Invite a conversation through a real, chosen channel.

Project stories use a simple repeatable structure: what prompted it, what he
tried, where it stands, and what he is considering next. Publish partial stories
without pretending they are finished case studies. Notes support a few sentences
or a video with a short written summary.

## Voice and draft opening

Conversational, curious, lightly sarcastic, candid. Avoid guru language, inflated
credentials, forced inspirational arcs, and repeated jokes at Karim's expense.

Proposed opening, for approval:

> Hi, I'm Karim. I like making things.
>
> Software, automations, experiments, and the occasional detour. This is where I
> share what I'm building and what I'm figuring out along the way.

Public copy may draw only on details Karim chooses to share. Health, therapy, and
other vulnerable interview material remain outside draft website copy.

Karim selected **Open Studio** from the direction previews. Its warm, open
composition connects a personal introduction to a varied body of work. Preserve
the meaning of the NewLad name and existing cube mark; replacing the logo is
outside this brief.

Pinned palette/material preference: white, black, natural wood, and plant green.
Interpret these as a coherent material vocabulary, not a stock photo of a plant
beside every project. Keep the spacious white ground, clear dark typography,
restrained green links, and material warmth of the selected preview.

**Portrait hero:** use Karim's supplied portrait, stored at
`src/images/karim-portrait.jpg`, in the selected circular crop. On desktop, pair
the personal introduction on the left with the portrait on the right, preserving
the selected layout's proportions. Preserve the original studio photograph's

Let the portrait establish who visitors are meeting. Give home automation,
Project Drift, and Fell for Love clear, individually recognizable project links

use a readable linear sequence and keep the first project close to the introduction.

Project selection should feel immediate and ordinary: visible links, clear
keyboard focus, and a restrained image response on hover/focus if useful. Never
hide project information behind that interaction. Project stories and the About
page can carry the same open spacing and personable typography; notes prioritize
comfortable reading.

## Interaction, range, and constraints

- Provide normal links, keyboard focus, semantic headings, readable contrast,
  and reduced-motion behavior. Exploration must work without animation or drag.
- Adapt from narrow phone screens to desktop. No important content should
  require hover, spatial guessing, or a particular display size.
- Show honest project states in words. Avoid stale-looking dates or invented
  activity to make the site appear busy.
- Design for one strong project or note as well as a growing archive. Hide empty
  sections; do not manufacture posts, counts, collaborators, or social proof.
- Keep the existing Astro content workflow unless publishing friction exposes a
  concrete need for another tool. CMS, accounts, comments, forum, newsletter, and
  a storefront are outside the proposed first version.
- Preserve existing links until replacement routes or redirects are planned.
